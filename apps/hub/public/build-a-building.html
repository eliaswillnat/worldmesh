Make a building

Your world's own front in the WorldMesh city.

Out in the city round the hall, every world has a block of its own: a plain white building with the world's door in it. Make a building of your own and it replaces the whole block, whether that is a temple, a shop, a hangar or a sign saying "come in". One face of it becomes the door visitors walk through, with everything a door does: the ripple as people come close, the live view, walking through.

Like characters, buildings stay where you host them, on your Sketchfab account or your AT Protocol (Bluesky) account. Each visitor's browser downloads yours from there. WorldMesh only keeps a link.

The one rule

Give the face visitors walk through a material named portal. Portal and portal.001 count too. The hub turns that face into your world's door, so what the material looks like doesn't matter.

No face called portal? The building still stands, with a standard door at its front. Or draw the door in the hub: see below.

What the file can be

  • One glTF binary (.glb) file with its textures inside, up to 10 MB.
  • Up to 150,000 triangles, with textures up to 2048 px.
  • No Draco or meshopt compression.
  • A still model: no animation, lights, sound or scripts.
  • Any size. It is fitted to its block for you.

How it is fitted

  • A block is 28 × 28 m and up to 28 m tall, twice the height of the plain blocks. It can also reach 2 m out into the street, for steps or a statue.
  • The building is turned so the portal faces the street, scaled evenly (never stretched) to fit, and stood with the bottom of the portal on the ground.
  • Once fitted, the portal must be at least 2 m wide and 2.5 m tall, so people fit through. A tall, narrow building leaves a small door; a squat one leaves a big door.
  • Keep the portal face flat, roughly a rectangle, and upright (facing within 30° of level).
  • Nothing of the building should stand in front of the portal face, or it shows through the door.

In Blender

  1. Model your building, or bring one in with File → Import.
  2. Pick the door: press Tab for Edit Mode, 3 for face select, and click the face (or faces).
  3. In Material Properties, add a material slot with +, click New, name the material portal and press Assign.
  4. Press Tab again, then File → Export → glTF 2.0, with Format set to glTF Binary (.glb) and compression left off.

Blender writes each material's faces as their own part of the file, so the door needs no separate object.

Made with an AI 3D generator such as Meshy or Tripo? Open its GLB in Blender, give the door face the portal material as above, and export. Their download links expire after a few days, so upload the file rather than linking to it.

Or draw the door in the hub

No Blender needed. Upload the GLB from My buildings, and once it is checked, choose Draw the door. Turn the model to the wall you want, switch to Draw, and drag across the wall from one corner of the door to the other. The hub shows the size the door will be on a block, and a grey plane for the street: anything below it goes underground. Use this door writes the door into the file as a face named portal, and that file is what gets uploaded.

A door drawn this way takes over from any portal face the file already had. Made from a model someone else shared, such as a free one from Sketchfab? Keep to its license, and credit its maker where it asks you to.

Put it on your world

  1. Sign in on WorldMesh, open your account and choose Buildings → Manage. Connect Sketchfab or your AT Protocol account, then upload your GLB there, or save a model already on it. Before uploading, the hub checks the file the way the city will and tells you how big its door will be, or what is wrong.
  2. Choose Put a building on your world, pick the building and your world from the list, and press Link. Nothing on your world's own site has to change.
  3. A person looks at the building before it stands in the city, usually within a day or two. My buildings shows how it went, and the reason if it wasn't approved.

Good to know

  • Approval is for that exact file. If you replace it on Sketchfab or in your AT Protocol account, the plain block comes back until the new file is reviewed.
  • Take off in My buildings brings the plain block back.
  • A world with a building of its own stands out in the city, even a multiplayer one that would otherwise have its door in the hall. The most entered worlds stand nearest the hall, the least entered furthest out.
  • Visitors can report a building, and reported buildings may be taken down. Use only models you have the rights to; see the terms.

Every detail, including how the portal face is found and fitted, is in the buildings spec. Connecting the world itself comes first: Connect a world.
